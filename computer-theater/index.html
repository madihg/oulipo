computer theater

Computer theater is live performance that takes the computer seriously - as stage, scene partner, apparatus, and sometimes author. This page is an initiative to explain it: what it is, how it started, and what it is made of.

It has no single origin. It has four braided lineages: the avant-garde breaking of the form, the networked body, screens brought onstage, and the screen itself becoming the stage. The graph below traces them - from Artaud to the algorave, from a 1966 armory in New York to the browser you are holding now.

click a node. a window opens. that gesture is part of the history.

Take any work of computer theater apart and the same seven parts are on the table. Different eras weighted them differently; none of them ever fully leaves the room.

A manifesto for computer theater

the body will be present.

the body will continue to be present.

the body cannot be removed, even as the digital takes over, even as the mind begins its upload.

the body is the new virus. the body is the oldest virus.

the body is here because the body is what we are.

the body as invasion. the body as contamination.

the body as vessel.

the body as original algorithm.

the body as the most addictive algorithm there is.

the body as the ultimate provocation of the mirror neuron.

the body as gift and trick, as wisdom and fool.

the body as instantiation, high precision.

the making real of dreams and fantasies.

the body as promise. the body as proof of work.

the body crosses; the form files it.

every border is a dramaturgy. every checkpoint is a stage.

we pray by clicking now. the machine keeps the vigil.

devotion did not leave the room. it changed seats.

a theater is a room that admits it is a room.

a browser is a room that forgets.

computer theater is the reminder.

even if the body disappears, the desire for the body will sit in the substrate of every model built after us.

even if the models forget, the body continues as an echo.

the body has ultimate romanticism. the body has ultimate classicism.

the body as end-all, be-all.

the body is here. the body isn’t going anywhere.

This manifesto is a working document, like the graph beside it. It borrows its pulse from the pieces in this kitchen: bodies made into input, borders made into forms, prayers made into clicks. It will be revised. The body will still be here.
