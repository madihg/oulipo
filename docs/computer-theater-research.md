# The Computer Theater Reading Room (ground truth for /computer-theater/)

Condensed from Halim's research doc (Jul 2026). Feeds the node blurbs in
computer-theater/index.html; scripts/check-computer-theater.mjs and review
agents treat this file as the source of truth.

Four braided lineages: (1) avant-garde "breaking of the form" (Artaud, Brecht, Beckett, Piscator, Futurists, Bauhaus); (2) networked/cybernetic body (Stelarc, telematics, motion capture); (3) screens and live video onstage (Wooster Group, Lepage, Builders Association, Katie Mitchell); (4) the screen itself as stage (net.art, desktop performance, live coding, algorithmic theatre). Best one-volume map: Steve Dixon, Digital Performance (MIT Press, 2007). Dixon explicitly roots digital performance in precursors from the Greek deus ex machina to Wagner's Gesamtkunstwerk and the historical avant-gardes (Futurism, Dada, Constructivism, Bauhaus).

Landmark origin: 9 Evenings: Theatre and Engineering, NYC, Oct 13-23 1966 - Bell Labs engineers + artists (Rauschenberg, Cage, Rainer et al.), first large-scale art-and-engineering performance. More analog/electronic than modern "computer". Computer-driven choreography: Cunningham LifeForms 1989->; chatbot/algorithmic theatre: Annie Dorsen 2010->.

TRACK 1 also includes Wagner (1813-1883, Gesamtkunstwerk, Bayreuth) as the precursor Dixon names. 9 Evenings venue: the 69th Regiment Armory, NYC. Artaud (1896-1948), Theatre of Cruelty, The Theatre and Its Double (1938, Eng. 1958); invoked in Katie Mitchell live-cinema scholarship. Brecht (1898-1956), epic theatre, Verfremdungseffekt, "The Radio as an Apparatus of Communication" (1932), Brecht on Theatre (Willett 1964); two-way apparatus; exposed machinery. Beckett (1906-1989): Krapp's Last Tape (1958) tape recorder as second character; Eh Joe (1965/66); Film (1965, Buster Keaton); radio plays (All That Fall 1957); Causey directed Beckett TV plays. Piscator (1893-1966): film/newsreel projection, treadmills, mechanized sets, Weimar 1920s; In Spite of Everything! (1925); Hoppla, We're Alive! (1927, multiple screens); Schweik (1928); coined "epic theatre" at Volksbuhne (1924-27) before Brecht; Das politische Theater (1929). Futurists: Marinetti/Settimelli/Corra "The Futurist Synthetic Theatre" (1915); "The Variety Theatre" (1913); speed, machines, simultaneity. Schlemmer (1888-1943): Triadic Ballet (premiered Stuttgart 1922, music later Hindemith; 3 acts, 3 dancers, 12 dances, 18 costumes); The Theater of the Bauhaus (1925, Eng. 1961); "Man and Art Figure". Fuller (1862-1928): Serpentine Dance (from ~1892), electric light + gels + projections, patented apparatus. Meyerhold (1874-1940): biomechanics, Constructivist stage. Svoboda (1920-2002): Laterna Magika, Expo 58 Brussels; projection + live performers.

TRACK 2: Stelarc (b. 1946): Fractal Flesh (Nov 1995, Paris/Helsinki/Amsterdam actuate his body in Luxembourg); Ping Body (1996, ping data drives muscles); ParaSite; Third Hand (1980-1998); Exoskeleton; 25 suspensions (1976-1988); Ear on Arm. Ascott (b. 1934): "Is There Love in the Telematic Embrace?" (Art Journal 1990); Telematic Embrace (ed. Shanken, UC Press 2003); content of telematic art = the human interaction. Galloway & Rabinowitz: Satellite Arts Project (1977, NASA satellites, two coasts); Hole-in-Space (Nov 1980, Lincoln Center NYC <-> LA Broadway dept store); Electronic Cafe (1984). Sermon (b. 1966): Telematic Dreaming (1992), ISDN + chroma-key bed; among the first graduates of Ascott's interactive arts course. Pask (1928-1996): Colloquy of Mobiles (1968), cybernetics. Cunningham (1919-2009): LifeForms 1989 (later DanceForms); Hand-drawn Spaces (SIGGRAPH 1998) + BIPED (1999, music Gavin Bryars) with Paul Kaiser + Shelley Eshkar (OpenEndedGroup); mocap figures on downstage scrim, live dancers behind. Troika Ranch (company founded early 1990s): Coniglio + Stoppiello, met CalArts 1989; MidiDancer suit; Isadora software 1999 (named for Isadora Duncan), now widely used. Also Palindrome (Robert Wechsler).

TRACK 3: Wooster Group (LeCompte, founded 1975): Brace Up! (1991, Three Sisters; 2003 version Paul Schmidt on video); Fish Story; House/Lights (1998/99, Stein x Olga's House of Shame, Kate Valk); To You, the Birdie! (Phedre) (2002); Hamlet (2007, vs 1964 Gielgud/Burton film). Lepage (b. 1957), Ex Machina (1994), La Caserne (1997) Quebec: Seven Streams of the River Ota; Elsinore (1995); The Far Side of the Moon (2000, music Laurie Anderson); Met Ring "the Machine" (2010-2012). Builders Association (Weems, founded 1994; ex-Wooster dramaturg/asst dir; OBIE-winning): Master Builder (1994); Jet Lag (1998, Diller + Scofidio); Alladeen (2003, motiroti, call centers); Super Vision (2005, dbox, dataveillance); Continuous City (2008); House/Divided (2012); book by Shannon Jackson & Weems (MIT 2015). Mitchell (b. 1964): The Waves (National Theatre 2006, video Leo Warner/Fifty Nine Productions); "live cinema"; actors + Foley construct film in real time; Fraulein Julie (Schaubuhne); ...some trace of her (2008); The Director's Craft (2009). Van Hove (b. 1958) + Versweyveld: Roman Tragedies (2007); Network (2017). Castorf: Volksbuhne intendancy 1992-2017, backstage live camera. George Coates Performance Works (SF, 1980s-90s): computer-generated stereoscopic projection.

TRACK 4: Mindy Seu (b. 1991): Cyberfeminism Index (2020 Rhizome commission; print Inventory Press 2022, 700+ entries); lecture-performance with AR app (Tommy Martinez); citation over authorship. Chia Amisola (b. 2000, Manila, they/them): internet & ambient artist; performs websites live, hundreds of windows, terminal + browser history exposed; Himala (2024); We Are Only Moving (panke.gallery Berlin 2023); My computer never asks... (Gray Area SF 2024); Philippine Internet Archive; Developh (2016); devotional computing. Lialina (b. 1971): My Boyfriend Came Back from the War (1996), HTML frames; A Vernacular Web; Turing Complete User; Digital Folklore (w/ Espenschied 2009). JODI (Heemskerk + Paesmans, founded 1994, site 1995): browser-breaking HTML; SOD (1999, Wolfenstein 3D mod); Untitled Game (1996-2001, Quake mod); %WRONG Browser; first Prix Net Art (Rhizome 2014). Shulgin (b. 1963): 386 DX (1998-2013), Windows 3.1 on 386, MIDI + TTS rock covers, clubs + streets; The Best of 386 DX (Staalplaat 2001); "world's first cyberpunk rock band" is a SELF-STYLED label. TOPLAP: born Hamburg bar 1am Sunday 15 Feb 2004 ("changing grammars" conference); ManifestoDraft "Show us your screens"; "algorave" coined by McLean + Collins 2011, first algorave London 2012; TidalCycles (McLean, 2006, Haskell). Dorsen: Hello Hi There (2010, two chatbots, Chomsky-Foucault 1971 debate); A Piece of Work (2013, Markov Hamlet); Yesterday Tomorrow (2015); The Great Outdoors (2017); Prometheus Firebringer (2023); MacArthur 2019; Guggenheim 2018. Machinima (from 1996, Diary of a Camper)/Second Life performance = cousin form.

WRITINGS: Dixon, Digital Performance (MIT 2007); Auslander, Liveness (1999; 3rd ed. 2023) - live and mediatized not ontologically opposed; Phelan, Unmarked (1993) - performance becomes itself through disappearance; Causey, Theatre and Performance in Digital Culture (2006) - "theatre has always been virtual", simulation -> embeddedness; Giannachi, Virtual Theatres (2004) - first book-length investigation (Blast Theory, Cunningham, Kac, Forced Entertainment, Stelarc, Orlan, JODI); Salter, Entangled (MIT 2010, foreword Peter Sellars); intermediality school: Chapple & Kattenbelt (2006), Bay-Cheng/Kattenbelt/Lavender/Nelson eds. Mapping Intermediality (2010); Kattenbelt: theatre as hypermedium; 'film provides the illusion of reality..., whereas theatre provides the reality of illusion'; Birringer, Media and Performance (1998); Schechner, Performance Studies; International Journal of Performance Arts and Digital Media.

ADJACENT: Laurie Anderson (b. 1947; United States Parts I-IV, 1983; music for Lepage's Far Side of the Moon), Blast Theory (1991->, Can You See Me Now? 2001), Eduardo Kac (b. 1962, telepresence/bio art), Forced Entertainment (1984->, Quizoola! 1996), Rimini Protokoll (2000->, Call Cutta 2005), Gob Squad (1994->, Kitchen 2007).

CAVEATS: "Firsts" are contested. 9 Evenings used analog/early electronic more than computers. Shulgin's "first cyberpunk band" self-styled. Some dates conflict (House/Lights 1998 vs 1999).
