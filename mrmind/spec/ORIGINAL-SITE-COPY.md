## framing (1950/2000)

source: `archive/_research/wayback/mrmind.com/20020223175502/site/intro/introduction/historical.html`

```
historical frame
 1950: The Turing Test
 2000:The Blurring Test
 Named for British mathematician, Alan Turing, The Turing Test is a milestone in the history of the relationship between humans and machines.
 The Turing Test is about machine progress; the computer convinces a human judge that it is, or is indistinguishable from, a human.
 Turning the Turing Test upside down, MRMIND challenges you to prove to him that you are human. Can you claim that your "human" attributes will forever be exclusively human?
 The Blurring Test is about human progress: Someday it might be important to convince our computers (and each other) that we are human.
```

```
disclaimer
```

## acknowledgements

source: `archive/_research/wayback/mrmind.com/20020819190921/site/about/credit/credits2.html`

```
Untitled Document
 Acknowledgements
 In 1980 I attended a lecture by Joseph Weizenbaum (the creator of
ELIZA) at M.I.T. His struggles over the response to ELIZA introduced me
to the topic. Over a decade later MRMIND was conceived (appropriately,
in conversation) as a response to the issues tested by the Turing Test;
I remarked that it would be more important and perhaps more difficult for
humans to prove to our computers that we are human.
 MRMIND came to life in his present form on the web because of David
Steuer's encouragement and early involvement in the project. The original
proposal was lucky to have caught the attention and the committed support
of WEBLAB in the persons of Marc Weiss and Barry Joseph. Their belief in
the project allowed it to survive the quicksand of non-profit sponsorship.
Chris Edwards from ATG came up with the current name, The Blurring Test.
Todd Ingalls' vision made MRMIND visible. Fred Simon brought him back to
life.
 Bots take on the personalit(ies) of their scriptors: Bear and JB as well
as myself. Many thanks to the thousands of (presumed) humans, including
my daughters, who have spoken to MRMIND since the summer of 1998 '
those conversations guided our scripting.
 Several close friends provided invaluable help throughout the project:
Carolyn Bates, Maud Lavin, Marla Steuer, and Alyce Dissette deserve
special mention. Two companies anonymously and generously provided
support; MRMIND and I are very grateful.
 Finally, MRMIND is dedicated to my favorite human, Richard Hollander.
 [credits]
```

## faq

source: `archive/_research/wayback/mrmind.com/20010411032109/site/hqquiz/faq/hqfaq.html`

```
HQquiz
 hq faq
 Answers to common questions about your HQ score:
 Q. What is an HQ score? What does HQ mean?
 A.HQ is HUMAN QUOTIENT. Unlike an IQ which is considered by many to be
 relatively constant throughout your life, your HQ fluctuates from moment to moment.
 Q.Can I change my HQ?
A. Yes. You are constantly responsible for your HQ.
 Q. What can I do to improve my HQ?
A. Strategies for affecting the HQ are highly individual...
 Q. What is a "high" HQ? What is a "low" HQ?
A. The relative value of any HQ score fluctuates according to societal trends.
 Q. What is my HQ?
A. The HQ score is not available to individuals.
 Q. How do I find out my HQ?
A. Sometimes it is possible with the aid of a mirror.
 Q. Can I forge my HQ?
A. Sure, it's done all the time.
 Q. Is the HQ test fair? Is the HQ test culture-blind?
A. The test is fair, opportunities to take the test may be interpreted as unfair.
 Q. How is my HQ score used?
A. It can be used for you; it can also be used against you.
 Q. Is my HQ part of my academic record?
A. Your HQ is unrelated to academic performance and is therefore not considered part of your academic record.
 Q. Can a perspective employer demand that I take an HQ test?
A. Some do, you probably just haven't noticed.
 Q. Are Government agencies allowed access to my HQ?
A. Government agencies consider HQ data irrelevant.
 Q. Do insurance companies have access to my HQ score?
A. Insurance companies are ignorant of HQ data.
 Q. Can my HQ be used in a court of law?
A. Your HQ is extremely important in a jury trial -- you should make every attempt to learn your attorney's HQ.
 Q. Do animals have an HQ?
A. On occasion.
 Q. What about stuffed animals?
A. In certain cases, stuffed animals may assist in the development of the HQ.
 Q. Does my computer have an HQ?
A. This varies from individual to individual.
 Q. How does the IQ affect the HQ?
A.The IQ has no bearing on the HQ.
 Q. What, exactly, does the HQ measure?
A. The HQ measures your behavior in relation to what you define to be humanity.
```
